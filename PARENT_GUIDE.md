# Parent Guide

## Ricardo's Summer Builder Program — For Dad

---

## What This Is

This is a 10-week summer enrichment program designed around Ricardo's candy business.

It is NOT a coding bootcamp. It is NOT school. It is NOT meant to prepare Ricardo for a CS exam.

The goal is one thing: **Ricardo ends summer believing he can build real things.**

Every project is connected to that belief. Programming is just the vehicle.

---

## Why This Approach

Ricardo is unusually positioned for a 13-year-old. He already has:

- A real product
- Real customers
- Real inventory
- Real money changing hands
- Real business decisions to make

Most coding courses teach kids to build fake things for imaginary companies. Ricardo gets to build real tools for his real business. That difference matters enormously for motivation and retention.

---

## Time Commitment

**4–6 hours per week.** Not per day. Per week.

This is designed to fit *into* summer, not consume it. Ricardo should have plenty of time for soccer, church, summer camp, friends, and family.

A typical week might look like:
- Two 2-hour sessions (Tuesday and Thursday afternoon, for example)
- One 90-minute session on a weekend

He should never feel behind. There is no deadline pressure.

---

## Your Role as Dad

### What to DO:
- Check GitHub once a week to see what Ricardo pushed
- Watch the Friday demo video he records
- Ask "What did you build this week?" (not "Did you finish?")
- Celebrate completions out loud
- Read his engineering journal when he shares it
- Be available for 10-minute "I'm stuck" conversations

### What NOT to do:
- Don't sit with him for every session
- Don't give him the answer when he's stuck
- Don't check on him every day
- Don't solve his bugs for him
- Don't push him to go faster

The goal is independence. If Ricardo has to call you every 20 minutes, the program hasn't done its job.

---

## How to Monitor Progress Remotely

Ricardo will maintain:

1. **GitHub repository** — All code is pushed here. You can see every commit.
2. **Progress tracker** — `TRACKER/progress.md` updated weekly
3. **Engineering journal** — `JOURNAL/` — Short weekly reflections
4. **Friday demo video** — A short (2–5 minute) screen recording each Friday

You don't need to grade these. You just need to acknowledge them.

A text that says "Watched your demo, that candy counter is awesome" goes a long way.

---

## The "Before Asking Dad" System

Ricardo has a checklist at `CHECKLISTS/before_asking_dad.md`.

Before he comes to you with a problem, he must:
1. Read the error message carefully
2. Search for the error online
3. Ask AI for help
4. Try at least one solution
5. Document what he tried

This is by design. The ability to get yourself unstuck is more valuable than any specific programming skill.

---

## AI Policy

Ricardo is allowed — encouraged — to use AI (Claude, ChatGPT, etc.).

But with one rule: **If he cannot explain the code in his own words, he has not learned it.**

The `RESOURCES/ai_policy.md` file gives him specific examples of good and bad AI use. Review it together.

The shortcut temptation is real. The antidote is asking him to explain things out loud. If he can explain it, he owns it.

## The Excel Workbook

Ricardo previously had a business system in Excel. That file should remain untouched as a backup.

The first major project of this program is rebuilding that system in Google Sheets — better, smarter, and connected to the cloud. That rebuild is his first real business software project.

---

## What Success Looks Like

By end of summer, Ricardo should have:

- [ ] A working GitHub repository with real projects
- [ ] A Google Sheets business system he built himself
- [ ] A real website for his candy business
- [ ] At least 4 working micro:bit projects
- [ ] A game he built
- [ ] A portfolio he's proud of
- [ ] A habit of figuring things out himself

If he has those things, this program worked.

---

## If Ricardo Gets Stuck (And He Will)

Getting stuck is not failure. Getting stuck is the job.

When he comes to you:
- Ask him to explain the problem first
- Ask "What have you tried?"
- Point him to `TROUBLESHOOTING/` if he hasn't looked there
- Sit with him for 10 minutes MAX
- Let him do the typing

You are a resource, not a rescue service.
