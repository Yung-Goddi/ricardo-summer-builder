# Customer Research

## Learn From the People Who Buy From You

**Time:** 2–3 hours spread across multiple days
**Difficulty:** Not coding — this is business research

---

## Why This Matters

Before you build your website, you need to know:

- What do customers actually want?
- What would make them order more?
- What products do they love? What's "meh"?
- How do they find you? How do they order now?
- What would make ordering easier?

You could guess all of this. Or you could just ask them.

This is called **customer research**. Every serious business does it. Most skip it and wonder why their products don't sell.

---

## The Plan

Interview **5 people** who have bought candy from you.

Friends, family members who've purchased, classmates, neighbors — anyone who is a real customer.

Each interview takes about 10–15 minutes. You can do one a day across a week, or knock out a few in one afternoon.

---

## Before the Interview

Tell them:
> "Hey, I'm building a website for my candy business this summer and I want to make it actually useful. Can I ask you a few questions about what would help? It'll only take like 10 minutes."

Most people will say yes.

**Bring a notebook.** Don't try to type while talking — you'll miss things. Write it down, type it up later.

---

## The Questions

Use these questions as a guide. If the conversation goes somewhere interesting, follow it. Don't stick to the script if they're telling you something useful.

### About Their Experience
1. "When was the last time you bought candy from me?"
2. "What made you decide to buy that particular candy?"
3. "Is there a candy you always want but I don't usually have?"
4. "What's your favorite thing I sell?"

### About Ordering
5. "How do you usually order from me or find out I'm selling?" (Do they text you? Just run into you? See a post?)
6. "Would you use a website to order candy ahead of time? Or do you prefer just buying when you see me?"
7. "What would make it easier to buy from me?"

### About Price and Value
8. "Do you feel like the prices are fair?" (It's okay if they say no — that's useful information)
9. "Would you pay a little more for a specific candy if I had it ready to go?"

### Open Ended
10. "Is there anything I could do better as a candy business?"
11. "If you could change one thing about how I sell candy, what would it be?"

---

## After Each Interview

Within an hour, write up your notes in the template below.

Don't wait until you've done all five — you'll forget details.

---

## Interview Notes Template

Copy this for each customer. Save them all in a Google Doc or in your journal.

```
CUSTOMER INTERVIEW
------------------
Date:
Name (or just "Customer 1", "Customer 2" etc. if they prefer):
How long they've been a customer:

What they said about my products:


What they said about ordering:


What would make them buy more:


Surprising things they said (things I didn't expect):


Improvements they suggested:


Overall impression:

```

---

## After All 5 Interviews

Read through all your notes and look for patterns:

- What did 3 or more people mention?
- What surprised you most?
- What's one change you'll definitely make?
- What's one thing you should add to your website?

Write a summary (1 page is enough) and save it as `business/customer_research_summary.md` in your GitHub.

---

## Use the Research

This research should influence:

**Your product list** — If 4 out of 5 people mentioned a candy you don't carry, that's a signal.

**Your website** — If most people said they'd use it, build it well. If most said "I just run into you," then the website is more of a marketing page and less of an order form.

**Your prices** — If multiple people thought something was too expensive, that's real feedback.

**Your system** — Maybe they all find out about you on Instagram, and you should focus on that.

---

## This Changes You

Something interesting happens when you interview customers.

You stop thinking "what do I want to sell?" and start thinking "what do they want to buy?"

That shift is huge. It's the difference between a hobby and a real business.

Most adult entrepreneurs take years to make that shift. You're doing it at 13.
